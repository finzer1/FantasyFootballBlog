4 Background

For the most part, Fantasy Football League sites provide predicted projected fantasy points for each player for that specific week. These predicted projections are based on a variety of factors, including past game performance, the player’s status (i.e., any potential injuries), and power ranking. It’s important to note that these predicted projections are not always entirely accurate, as certain factors (e.g., an opponent ramming into a player, causing the player to be injured) cannot be accurately predicted. With that being said, how accurate will Week 7 projected fantasy points be in comparison to actual fantasy points earned?

5 The Present Study

In the present study, I examine my own Fantasy Football League roster (i.e., starting and benched players) and compare projected fantasy points to actual fantasy points earned during Week 7.

For my hypothesis, I hypothesize that actual fantasy points will be within five fantasy points of the projected fantasy points. My hypothesis is justified because I don’t expect the predictions to be completely accurate, but rather the estimates will be in proximity to the actual fantasy points earned. Football can be unpredictable at times, but it is possible to accurately project fantasy points or at least close to the actual fantasy points.

6 Method

To test the hypothesis, I will examine my Fantasy Football League roster (i.e., starting and benched players) and compare projected fantasy points to actual fantasy points earned during Week 7. Predicted projections of fantasy points were implemented from DraftSharks, while actual fantasy points earned were implemented from NFL Fantasy.

I will evaluate forecasting accuracy (i.e., how close predicted projected fantasy points are to actual fantasy points earned) and overconfidence (i.e., if predictions are too close to the mean).

Specifically, my 13 players (i.e., not counting injured or players on a bye week) are classified as categorical, while my projected and actual fantasy points are classified as continuous.

Results

Based on the results, the standard deviation for projected points was 4.4, while the standard deviation for actual fantasy points earned was 7.7. This depicts that DraftSharks’ forecasts were showing signs of overconfidence or being too close to the mean. This suggests that DraftSharks (potentially) tends to underestimate the range of possible outcomes.

Now, let’s take a look at this from the perspective of each player:

Code 
petersenlab::accuracyOverall(
  predicted = df$Projected_Points,
  actual = df$Actual_FPTS,
  dropUndefined = TRUE
)

Results

These results further prove that DraftSharks tends to underestimate rather than overestimate their projections. But more importantly, the average magnitude of error (i.e., MAE) was approximately 3.27. This number falls within the range of my hypothesis, which allows individual errors to increase by up to 5 fantasy points. So, on average, the actual fantasy points were within 3.27 fantasy points of the predicted projected fantasy points.

8 Discussion

In this article, I hypothesize that actual fantasy points will be within five fantasy points of the projected fantasy points. Additionally, I also examined DraftSharks’ forecasting accuracy and potential signs of overconfidence. From the data, my hypothesis was partially correct, as DraftSharks was shown to exhibit signs of overconfidence while also tending to underestimate the range of possible outcomes. Regarding statistical significance, due to my small sample size, the results of my study would not be statistically significant. However, the practical significance of DraftSharks is shown to have practical significance. On an individual level (i.e., for each player), practical significance was shown to be less consistent/predictable.

An alternative explanation for why players’ projected predictions weren’t as close could be due to the unpredictability of certain factors, such as weather or injuries, during the game.

9 Strengths and Limitations

The study had several strengths. First, I implemented several measuring metrics through my very own Fantasy Football League roster. By implementing a visual aid, readers can easily see the differences between projected and actual fantasy points earned.

The study also had limitations. First, my small sample size (n = 13) severely limits my generalizability. Additionally, unpredictable variables such as injuries could explain why some of my values are more extreme than others. Lastly, I only accessed one period rather than accessing overall trends. Next time, accessing several weeks and increasing the sample size would be a notable possibility for future work to consider.

10 Conclusion

Predicted projections on Fantasy Football sites can serve as a decently reliable source for predicting fantasy football points. Although it’s essential to remember that projections aren’t always completely accurate, they are generally accurate (e.g., within 1-5 fantasy points of projections). Additionally, these projections don’t take into account potential weather issues or injuries that may occur during the game, which can severely impact the number of fantasy points your player will obtain.
