4 Background

4.1 The Present Study

In the present study, I examined quarterbacks’ past performance (i.e., passing yards) and whether it can predict whether they will throw for 300+ yards in the next game. Research Question: Can a quarterback’s passing yards in the previous game predict whether they will throw for 300 or more passing yards in the next game? Hypothesis: Quarterbacks who throw more passing yards in their previous game are more likely to throw 300 or more passing yards in their next game. Prediction: A quarterback’s passing yards in the previous game will accurately predict whether they achieve a 300-yard passing performance in the next game.

5 Method

To test the hypothesis, I first examined whether a cutoff-based classification system, combined with diagnostic accuracy metrics and ROC analysis, can assess how well quarterbacks’ passing yards from their previous game can predict a 300-yard passing performance in the next game.

6.1 Accuracy at a Particular Cutoff

Providing the accuracy for predicting whether or not the Quarter Back will get 300+ passing yards in their next game:

Code 
petersenlab::accuracyAtCutoff(
  predicted = player_stats_weekly_example$passing_yards_priorGame, # predictor variable
  actual = player_stats_weekly_example$pass_300_yds, # outcome variable
  cutoff = 300
)

Based on the results, using the quarterback’s passing yards from the previous game to predict whether they would throw for 300+ yards resulted in an overall accuracy of 75.66% but, but only about 16.75% of games result in 300+ yards…

Accuracy by chance (i.e., randomly guessing) rate sits at a 72.02% while the accuracy predicting from base rate (i.e., giving an answer based on what has the better chance at happening) sits at a 83.25%.

Additionally, the sensitivity is low (i.e., the model will identity only about one in four 300+ games), but the specificity is high (i.e., meaning it’s very good at pointing out the games that won’t exceed 300+ yards). As a result, this gives us a balanced accuracy of 0.565 so just slightly above better than chance.

Lets lower the cutoff to 100 passing yards from their previous game:

Code 
petersenlab::accuracyAtCutoff(
  predicted = player_stats_weekly_example$passing_yards_priorGame, # predictor variable
  actual = player_stats_weekly_example$pass_300_yds, # outcome variable
  cutoff = 100
)

Based on the results above, the sensitivity increased significantly while the specificity decreased tremendously therefore increasing the chance of more false positives. This indicates that having a low cutoff puts too much faith into predicting rare outcomes which we do not want (i.e., sensitivity = true positive rates–identifying actual 300 yard games vs specificity = true negative rates–identifying non-300-yard games). Additionally the balanced accuracy was raised slightly to about 0.572. So overall, still not great results.

Let’s keep trying and raise the cutoff one more time to 150 passing yards from their previous game to see if we get better results:

Code 
petersenlab::accuracyAtCutoff(
  player_stats_weekly_example$passing_yards_priorGame, # predictor variable
  actual = player_stats_weekly_example$pass_300_yds, # outcome variable
  cutoff = 150
)

The sensitivity is lowered by almost 10% and the specificity is raised similarily. But, we got a balanced accuracy of 0.595! So far, a cutoff of 150 yards has appeared to demonstrate the most statistical trade off!

9 Strengths and Limitations

Strengths: The study used thousands of quarterbacks, which contributed to greater reliability and generalizability (i.e., a large sample). The data used was real-world data, from all the way to 1999 to 2024.

Limitations: The study did not incorporate other similar research to support the predictions, hypotheses, or findings. The results of the study may be due to different external factors, such as weather, physical health (e.g., injury status), and/or the defensive strength of the other team. The study assessed only quarterbacks, so the data are generalizable only to quarterbacks, not to other positions. It’s important to consider that the study’s statistical significance may be due to an overly large sample size.

9.1 Conclusion

If the study were to be repeated, I would incorporate more variables (e.g., tackles, catches, passing yards, etc) and cover other football positions, rather than just quarterbacks, to make the findings more generalizable.
